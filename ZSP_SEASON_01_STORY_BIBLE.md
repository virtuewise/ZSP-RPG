# ZSP_SEASON_01_STORY_BIBLE.md

**ZIMBABWE: THE SHADOW PROTOCOL — Season One: THE BLACK LEDGER**
Genre: Espionage thriller · Military action · Conspiracy · Crime · Mystery
Setting: modern-day fictionalised Zimbabwe, 2026
Style: a continuous premium action-thriller series; every mission is an episode.

**Central idea.** The person hunting you might be the only one trying to save you. The person protecting you might be the reason everything went wrong.

**Disclaimer (required, always visible):** This is a work of fiction. Zimbabwe is used as
recognisable geography. Every named character, unit, agency and organisation is invented.
Nothing here depicts, accuses, or implies any real person, party, ministry, service,
business or security force. Any resemblance is coincidental.

**Production tone.** Restraint. Short sentences. Dust, diesel, rain, marble, corrugated
iron, good whisky in bad rooms. The state is never a cartoon; the conspiracy is never
cartoonishly evil. The enemy is a reasonable person with an unreasonable mandate.

**Visual language.** Dusk amber and charcoal; msasa trees; long shadows; interiors that
feel borrowed from three different decades of money. Music: sub-bass dread with
mbira-inflected motifs that decay, never resolve. VO: dry, tired, precise.

---

## 1. The world

Zimbabwe, 2026. For years an underground intelligence network known as **THE SHADOW NETWORK**
has influenced events across the country. It has no official headquarters, no recognised
leadership, and no public existence. It operates through intermediaries: private security
companies, diplomats, ministries' back rooms, import-finance deals, and international
intelligence contacts. Its public face is competence. Its private product is deniability.

At the centre sits a classified programme called **THE SHADOW PROTOCOL**.

It was originally built to protect Zimbabwe against a genuine national-security threat —
an arms network moving through the region toward a catastrophic target. The protocol was
a predictive weapon: it identified people as threats, predicted their movements, and
coordinated pre-emptive responses. It worked.

Then it was compromised.

Its original purpose was abandoned. Its operators discovered that a list of "threats" is
also a list of enemies. The protocol began identifying citizens as threats on information
that could be manipulated — because the information could be manipulated.

A copy of everything — identities, operations, money, photographs, route data, human
assets — leaves the network as **THE BLACK LEDGER**: a classified archive. A few hours later, **Deputy Director Emmanuel Mhara**, the senior intelligence officer who carried it out, disappears.

Before vanishing he sends one encrypted message to a former operative, Tinashe Mukasa,
who left the service three years earlier:

> **"They have activated the protocol. Run."**

That is where the game begins.

## 2. Main character

### TINASHE MUKASA, 34 — former elite intelligence operative
> "The service lied to me. So did the politicians. I'd like, for once, to know whether the truth does."

Quiet, analytical, strategic, emotionally guarded. He led **Operation Silent Gate**, a
mission to stop an arms shipment reaching an unidentified organisation. Something went
catastrophically wrong: his entire team was killed; the officer who authorised the mission
— Deputy Director Collin Mavhunga — disappeared; Tinashe was blamed and forced out. He
drove trucks. He tried to forget.

What he does not know: **Operation Silent Gate was part of the original Shadow Protocol.**
And the shipment was a frame. The real mission was him.

**Traits:** notices exits before men; eats standing up; never loads a weapon he isn't
holding; cannot sleep in rooms with doors that lock inward; remembers everything he was
trained to remember, which is the problem.

**Want:** to know what really happened.
**Need:** to accept that he was never the guilty one — and that innocence does not cancel
complicity.
**Flaw:** he trusts procedure over people, until people are the only thing left.
**Gift:** he was the protocol's control case. It knows him better than his mother did.

## 3. The supporting cast

### AMARA CHIKOWORE — intelligence analyst
Tinashe's former intelligence contact. Brilliant at surveillance, signals, digital
investigation and counter-forensics. She helps him because she is the only person still
watching the same files.

**Secret:** the night Operation Silent Gate failed, Amara had already seen the targeting
file. She flagged the anomaly and was ordered to stop. She copied the Silent Gate
after-action before it was sealed. She has been carrying it for three years. She stayed
because leaving would have meant nothing; she is not brave, she is exact.
She does not tell Tinashe because she is the only living proof the protocol works — and
the protocol marked her "asset, not threat."

### GENERAL DANIEL MUPONDA — the mysterious director
Powerful former intelligence director who knows where the protocol came from. Claims he
wants to protect the country. Refuses to explain why Tinashe's team was sacrificed.

**Secret:** he authorised Silent Gate believing the real target was the arms network. He
was handed a file he never asked for: an operational directive to "terminate a security
leak within the team." The leak was Tinashe. Muponda could not stop it; stopping it would
have confirmed the leak was real and would have detonated the service. He kept Tinashe
"alive and disgraced" instead. It was not mercy; it was triage.

Muponda is the person hunting Tinashe — and the only person trying to save him, badly,
late, and on his own terms.

### VICTOR NHARI — the hunter
A highly skilled private military contractor tasked with retrieving the Black Ledger.
Knows Tinashe personally. Anticipates every move he makes — because the protocol predicts
him.

**Secret:** Victor Nhari was Tinashe's teammate. Vehicle section, callsign Sable. The
official report says Victor died in the same ambush. He did not. He was captured,
interrogated by Meridian Strategic Group, and turned. He has spent three years hunting the
man he believes left his team to die — and feeding Meridian the parameters that keep
Tinashe alive just long enough to be caught useful.

He is hunting Tinashe personally. He is also, in a way, waiting for him to arrive.

### TAFADZWA DUBE — the journalist
Investigative reporter at **The Ledger**, a small independent newspaper. Discovers a
pattern: missing persons, payments routed through shell import companies, a security
contractor that never publicly bids. He wants to expose everything regardless of the
consequences — the consequences include him.

Tafadzwa is sincere, brave, and one leak away from being another missing person. He is
the player's public voice; the story's moral oxygen; and, sometimes, the match that burns
the room down before anyone's ready.

### ADRIAN VOSS — the unknown mastermind
International businessman with connections to private security, intelligence contractors and
financial interests. Nobody knows whether he created the Shadow Protocol or simply took
control of it.

Voss does not attend meetings in person; he attends as a calendar invite, a lawyer, and a
convoy. He bought the protocol the way he bought banks. Whether he authored it or
*converted* it, he treats it as a product: a predictive engine for national behaviour,
packaged for governments and favoured elites.

He is the kind of man who would describe genocide as "risk mitigation."

## 5. The 12 episodes

### Episode 01 — THE MESSAGE
**Location:** Harare (CBD  ->  Avondale). **Cast:** Tinashe, Amara (voice/text), Deputy Director Mhara (missing).
**Logline:** A ghost returns for six words.
- Cold open: rain on a truck window; a radio crackles; Tinashe drives past a checkpoint that isn't supposed to exist.
- A device pings in a locker at Mbare bus terminus. Metadata: source is a dead man. Message: *"They have activated the protocol. Run."*
- He tries to wipe it; a Meridian patrol is already at his building. He kills no one; he slips a window; he is now a person of interest with a phone that keeps pinging.
- **Objectives:** reach the dead drop; avoid the patrol; contact Amara or run alone.
- **Cinematic:** long take, handheld, no music until the message clears. The city is alive with cameras.
- **Player decision:** trust Amara (she answers) / trust Muponda (unknown signal) / trust no one and dump the phone.
- **SIS hook:** the protocol begins modelling him the moment he fails to run.
- **Consequences:** protecting the phone raises Meridian's readiness; destroying it loses Amara's early intel.

### Episode 02 — DEAD SIGNAL
**Location:** Chitungwiza (safehouses). **Cast:** Tinashe, Amara, Deputy Director Mhara (via records).
- Tinashe and Amara reconstruct Mhara's last 72 hours. His car was abandoned with the engine running. His house was searched by professionals who took nothing and left nothing.
- A shopkeeper saw his phone being handed to a man with a Meridian badge that didn't scan.
- **Objectives:** recover the phone; identify the handler; plant a tracker.
- **Cinematic:** a long, quiet surveillance sequence in a night market; the city breathes; nobody helps anybody.
- **Player decision:** involve the police (fatal mistake) / involve Tafadzwa (burns the source) / involve Muponda (saves him; marks him).
- **SIS hook:** every citizen who helps becomes a "possible facilitator" in the model.

### Episode 03 — THE SAFEHOUSE
**Location:** Avondale/Msasa safe-house network. **Cast:** Tinashe, Amara, Tafadzwa (first appearance), Meridian strike team.
- The Avondale safehouse is hit at 04:10. Amara is out. Tinashe arrives at the wrong moment.
- Tafadzwa Dube is at the safehouse because he was tipped off; he is shot in the shoulder by a Meridian cleaner.
- Tinashe carries him out through the canal. The city is lit by a helicopter.
- **Objectives:** escape the net; treat Tafadzwa; decide whether he lives or dies.
- **Cinematic:** one continuous 4-minute escape; no jump cuts; rain, concrete, a dog that doesn't bark.
- **Player decision:** leave Tafadzwa / drag him / call Amara for surgical extraction (saves him, marks safehouse).
- **SIS hook:** carrying a casualty changes Meridian's asset valuation of him: *target + ballast.*
- **Consequences:** Tafadzwa lives  ->  Meridian adds him to the file; Tafadzwa dies  ->  Tafadzwa's death becomes Amara's fuse.

### Episode 04 — BROKEN TRUST
**Location:** Harare / Bulawayo road / the Directorate. **Cast:** Tinashe, Directorate interrogators, Amara, General Muponda (first voice contact).
- Mhara's phone holds a single encrypted folder and a photograph of Tinashe himself, dated three years ago.
- Tinashe is stopped at an NSD checkpoint and taken in for "routine verification." His old clearance is revoked in real time.
- Amara breaks him out using a Directorate contractor truck that goes cold at the river crossing.
- **Objectives:** survive interrogation; learn who revoked the file; follow the money.
- **Cinematic:** a fluorescent room; a clock that is always slightly wrong; a man who is reading from a script Tinashe wrote.
- **Player decision:** tell the truth / lie better / say nothing (safest; costs Amara).
- **SIS hook:** the protocol updates his profile: *hostile, isolated, still valuable.*

### Episode 05 — GHOSTS OF HARARE
**Location:** Harare (childhood district) / farm outside Chinamhora. **Cast:** Tinashe, Amara, pensioned sergeant / a survivor.
- Tinashe finds a pay record for the Sable section — his own team. The recipient bank account is "MERIDIAN MSG-09".
- He visits a survivor: a man so damaged he speaks in third person. He says only: *"You weren't the target. You were the delivery."*
- **Objectives:** identify delivery/target; decode the Silent Gate after-action; avoid Meridian's new pursuit team.
- **Cinematic:** a farm with one good chair and three broken ones; a thunderstorm that never quite lands.
- **Player decision:** open the after-action with Amara / with Tafadzwa / alone.
- **SIS hook:** reading the file triggers a "witness sweep" around everyone who ever knew him.

### Episode 06 — THE INFORMANT
**Location:** Bulawayo (rail yard / hotel). **Cast:** Tinashe, Tafadzwa, the Informant (deputy director's bodyman), Victor Nhari (first confirmed appearance).
- The informant is a low-level Meridian archivist who wants out. He hands over a page of the Black Ledger: a list of "threats" with disposal dates.
- Victor Nhari walks in. He does not kill Tinashe. He kills the informant. He looks at Tinashe like a man recognising a grave.
- **Objectives:** get the page out; survive Victor; decide who to trust with it.
- **Cinematic:** a hotel bar; too many mirrors; a glass that is always half-empty.
- **Player decision:** give the page to Amara (hide) / Tafadzwa (publish) / Muponda (buried).
- **SIS hook:** the page is a decoy. The real ledger is elsewhere.
- **Consequences:** publishing the page triggers Episode 07's border move earlier and hotter.

### Episode 07 — THE CROSSING
**Location:** Mutare  ->  Beitbridge (border corridor). **Cast:** Tinashe, Amara, Tafadzwa, Meridian border team.
- The page points to a smuggling corridor: vehicles flagged in Bulawayo appear in Mutare with different plates.
- Tinashe learns the Silent Gate arms shipment moved through this exact route. It was never seized. It was *delivered*. To someone unknown.
- He can seize the current shipment, destroy it, or let it pass and follow it.
- **Objectives:** infiltrate the border warehouse; decide the arms' fate; escape east.
- **Cinematic:** headlights splitting dawn; the border fence humming; a customs officer who sleeps with one eye open.
- **Player decision:** seize & leverage / destroy (saves lives; burns leverage) / follow (learns the buyer).
- **SIS hook:** whichever he does, the protocol predicts the next move and pre-loads it.

### Episode 08 — NO WITNESSES
**Location:** Harare (safehouse) / Chitungwiza. **Cast:** Tinashe, Amara, Tafadzwa's source, Meridian cleaner team.
- A source arranging a meeting with Tafadzwa disappears. Her desk is clean; her chair is warm; her phone is in a river.
- Muponda warns Tinashe through a dead channel: the exchange is a "kill box." He does not say who else is in it.
- **Objectives:** get to the meeting site first; extract Amara; find the missing source.
- **Cinematic:** a car park with too many cars; a bus that arrives early; a mother calling her son's name.
- **Player decision:** obey Muponda (save Amara; lose the source) / defy Muponda (save the source; lose Amara) / trust neither (save no one, learn everything).
- **SIS hook:** saving the source reveals Amara was at the Silent Gate debrief — the secret she has kept for three years.

### Episode 09 — THE BETRAYAL
**Location:** Harare (safehouse) / the Directorate. **Cast:** Tinashe, General Muponda, Amara, Tafadzwa.
- The protocol's "black box" is recovered. It contains the Silent Gate directive: *Terminate a security leak within the Sable section before Phase Two.*
- Muponda does not deny it. He explains: the target was the arms network; the leak was real; Tinashe was not the target; he was the *container* — the only operative with the clearance to hold Phase Two.
- Then the building is hit. Muponda hands Tinashe to Meridian — or appears to.
- **Objectives:** survive the grab; identify the real deliverer; choose who to believe.
- **Cinematic:** a convoy, a hospital, a prayer that is never finished.
- **Player decision:** believe Muponda (run with him) / believe Amara (run with her) / believe no one (run alone, with the box).
- **SIS hook:** trusting Muponda raises "Directorate Trust" but lowers "Meridian Confidence" — the system adapts on the contradiction immediately.
- **Consequences:** believing Muponda keeps Amara alive; believing Amara keeps Tinashe alive; believing no one keeps the truth alive.

### Episode 10 — BLACKOUT
**Location:** Harare (nationwide). **Cast:** Tinashe, Amara, Tafadzwa, Meridian assault teams, Victor.
- Coordinated failure: power, water, mobile networks, airside. Meridian executes a "clean sweep" protocol across the city.
- Tafadzwa's newsroom is hit. Amara's laptop is stolen. Tinashe is nearly taken in a hospital corridor.
- Victor corners him on a roof. Victor does not shoot. He says: *"Run better."*
- **Objectives:** survive the blackout; protect Amara; keep the box alive.
- **Cinematic:** an entire city lit only by fire trucks and lightning; a helicopter with no sirens.
- **Player decision:** get Amara to the border / get Tafadzwa to print / get the box to Muponda.
- **SIS hook:** the protocol uses the blackout to reclassify every survivor as an asset or a liability. The player chooses.

### Episode 11 — THE PROTOCOL
**Location:** Victoria Falls (Meridian data facility) / Masvingo (Great Zimbabwe ruins, fallback). **Cast:** Tinashe, Amara, Victor (ally?), Voss (voice).
- The box opens in a facility built inside a casino. The Shadow Protocol was never a programme to protect Zimbabwe. It was a national stability engine: identify threats, predict them, pre-empt them.
- It worked for two years. Then it was sold.
- Tinashe finds his file:
  > **SUBJECT: TINASHE MUKASA — STATUS: ACTIVE THREAT — DIRECTIVE: TERMINATE**
  Date: three years ago. Operation Silent Gate was not a failed mission. It was the execution phase.
- **Objectives:** enter the facility; extract the ledger; leave with Amara or with the truth.
- **Cinematic:** a casino with no windows; a vault painted like a bull; the falls audible only when the fans stop.
- **Player decision:** destroy the ledger (protect the country, bury the truth) / leak it (expose the network) / keep it (become the new custodian).
- **SIS hook:** the protocol tries to rewrite him using his own file. Whatever the player chooses, the system learns.

### Episode 12 — THE LAST DIRECTIVE
**Location:** Victoria Falls / Zimbabwe plateau. **Cast:** Tinashe, Amara, Victor, Adrian Voss, General Muponda.
- Tinashe confronts Voss. Voss is polite. He explains the protocol's new deployment: other countries, other files, same predictive logic. Zimbabwe was the **pilot**.
- Muponda arrives with a Directorate team. He offers Tinashe a place inside the rebuilt service. Refuse. He does not blink.
- Victor stands at the ridge. He does not fire. He transmits: *"Tinashe — if you're reading this, you've already made the mistake they wanted you to make."*
- **Objectives:** choose the ending of Season One.
- **The three endings (all canon-valid):**
  - **Expose** — Tafadzwa prints. The protocol is named. The country survives, damaged, awake.
  - **Control** — Amara takes the box. The network is dismantled from inside. Nobody claps.
  - **Burn** — the facility burns. Nothing is published. Voss escapes with the proof; the truth survives only in Tinashe's head.
- **End card:** THE SHADOW PROTOCOL IS STILL ACTIVE.

**Season One does not resolve the story. It answers one question — *was Operation Silent Gate a failure?* — and introduces a worse one: *who else is in the file?***

## 6. Twists and reveals (order is contractual)

1. The message came from a man already dead.
2. Mhara did not disappear; he was **retired**.
3. Amara was at the Silent Gate debrief — and survived because she was "an asset, not a threat."
4. The Silent Gate arms shipment was never seized; it was delivered.
5. Victor Nhari is alive.
6. The protocol predicted Tinashe's entire arc before he began it.
7. Muponda authorised the mission but not the execution — or chose not to know.
8. Voss did not create the protocol. He **bought** it.
9. Zimbabwe was the pilot; global deployment is in Phase Two.
10. Tinashe's file says ACTIVE THREAT. It was opened three years ago. It has never been closed.
11. The final message is from Victor — and it is not a rescue.
12. The Black Ledger is not evidence of a conspiracy. It is a **shopping list**.

## 7. Player decisions and the Adaptive Enemy Doctrine

**Recommended system name (player-facing): SHADOW INTELLIGENCE — Adaptive Enemy Doctrine.**
The engine name (SIS) stays internal.

The doctrine is not "enemies get stronger." It is: *the game remembers how you hunted, and the next episode is the argument against it.* It changes enemy preparation, mission objectives, relationships, intelligence and future events — under a fixed budget, under non-negotiable invariants, replays from a hash-chained ledger.

### Decision axes and persistent consequences

| Axis | Exemplary choice | Example consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Trust | Trust Amara / Muponda / no one | Amara exposed  ->  later rescue; Muponda trusted  ->  Directorate cover but Meridian escalation; no trust  ->  Meridian declassifies Tinashe as a hostile unknown. |
| Approach | Same entrance, same lane | Meridian reinforces it; cameras increase; routes narrow. |
| Violence | Loud weapons / stealth | Enemy armour/detection up; civilians flagged as threats; Tafadzwa's story changes. |
| Mercy | Spare Victor / spare informants | Victor may falter; informants reappear as witnesses — or as liabilities. |
| Publication | Publish early / leak late / bury | Tafadzwa's survival; Meridian heat; public pressure; Muponda's limits. |
| The shipment | Seize / destroy / follow | Leverage over Meridian; moral cost; the buyer's identity delayed or revealed. |
| The ledger | Expose / control / burn | Season One ending; Season Two factions. |
| Amara's secret | Confront / forgive / exploit | Alliance strength; the safehouse; the archive decryption. |

### Hard invariants
- Operation Silent Gate and the protocol are **always** revealed.
- Tinashe's file is **always** found.
- At least one ending is **always** available.
- Voss survives to plant Season Two (he may be wounded, captured, or humiliated — never resolved).
- Victor's final message happens in every playthrough.

### SIS integration with the 12 episodes
Every episode emits behaviour events (approach, combat, stealth, interrogation, trust, exposure, kills) into the ledger. The adaptation budget (max 10 points per mission) applies only to conditions: security, detection, armour, reinforcements. It never edits the confrontation, the archive, the file, or the endings.

## 8. Season Two — THE GLOBAL FILE (setup)

- **The Consortium** moves the protocol to Phase Two. Three other countries are pilot sites. Zimbabwe is the burn file.
- **Victor** resurfaces in a different uniform. He cannot save Tinashe; only himself.
- **Amara** discovers the protocol's source code is not a program; it is a governance decision with a switch.
- **Tafadzwa's** newspaper becomes the only mirror large enough to hold the story; it is bought.
- **Muponda** is arrested for a crime he did not commit, and knows it.
- **Mhara** sends one last message — from inside the programme.
- **The Consortium's chairman** is a name that will matter: **Dr. L. Okoro** (invented), a development economist with a private security arm and a Nobel she very much does not want.
- **Final shot:** a server room in Geneva; a new file opens; the subject line reads **SUBJECT: ZIMBABWE** — and it is not Tinashe.

## 10. Production notes

- **Cinematography:** 35mm texture, handheld in crisis, composed in calm; no Dutch angles; rain everywhere but never convenient.
- **Sound:** the click of a magazine, the hum of a generator, market noise bleeding through concrete, a mbira pattern that decays into a siren.
- **VO:** dry, economical; Zimbabwean voice actors, dialect authentic, not theatrical; subtitles in English and Shona/Ndebele where relevant.
- **Dialogue:** no speeches longer than three lines; every line either advances the investigation or reveals the speaker.
- **Music:** composer brief: *"Dread you can hum."*
- **Rating:** 16+; violence is consequential, never stylised for pleasure; no torture porn.
- **Representation:** Zimbabweans are competent, varied, funny, flawed, and never exoticised; Zimbabwe is not a backdrop.

---

## 11. Non-negotiables

1. No real Zimbabwean person, agency, ministry, party, business, police, army, intelligence or political figure is depicted, implied, accused or alluded to.
2. No real country's abuse is used as shorthand for Zimbabwe's politics.
3. No ethnic group is weaponised as a faction.
4. The state is fictional; the geography is real; the people are invented; the crimes are invented.
5. The game's moral spine is pro-Zimbabwean, pro-truth, and anti-secrecy — not anti-any-people.

## 14. THE OPERATIVE IDENTITY SYSTEM (OIS)

**Status:** adopted for Season One. **Design principle:** personal connection without replacing the protagonist, the timeline, or the writing.

Tinashe Mukasa remains the fixed protagonist — his face, history, personality, body and voice are non-negotiable. The player personalises **how the campaign identifies them**, never who Tinashe is.

### 14.1 What the player chooses

| Field | Used for | Default | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Display name** | Menus, mission records, campaign profile, save slots | `Tinashe Mukasa` | 28 chars, profanity-filtered, homoglyph-safe |
| **Operational callsign** | Intercepts, enemy intel files, briefings, debriefs, SIS audit panel, Victor's transmission | `SHADOW-07` | 12 chars, validated, no empties |
| **Optional nickname** | Selected conversational barks where text supports it; never VO | empty | 14 chars; treated as plain display text |
| **Campaign profile** | Dossier screen: campaign, episodes completed, decisions, achievements | auto-generated | read-only |

Sample in-game profile:

> **PLAYER OPERATIVE PROFILE — SHADOW-07**
> Display name: **NIGHTFALCON**
> Campaign protagonist: **Tinashe Mukasa**
> Operational callsign: **SHADOW-07**
> Campaign: **The Black Ledger**
> Identity registered · Episode 03 of 12

### 14.2 How the identity appears

- **Mission briefings / debriefs** reference the callsign, not the real name ("SHADOW-07 went dark at the river crossing").
- **Enemy intel files** record the callsign. The protocol writes the player's chosen handle into the file. *This is the SIS telling the player: you are in the dossier too.*
- **Contacts** recognise the callsign as the campaign progresses. Trust/fear tiers are shown in the dossier as reputation words, not numbers.
- **Victor's transmission** is templated:
  > *"Tinashe, SHADOW-07 just went dark on the grid. If you're reading this, you've already made the mistake they wanted you to make."*
  (Display string substitution only; VO, if any, is callsign-free.)
- **SIS audit panel** becomes diegetic: the player sees their own field report, not a debug menu.
- **Later seasons** carry display name, callsign and reputation forward.

### 14.3 What the player does NOT choose (Season One)

- Tinashe's **appearance** is fixed. No face, hair, scar, height, build or voice options in Season One.
- Tinashe's **personality, history and trauma** are fixed; the protocol predicted them, not you.
- **The Lie** — the Silent Gate kill-order — is fixed canon.
- **The endings** are reachable by any name; the text does not change.

Clothing, weapons, equipment and abilities remain player-customisable from Season One; only the body/face are locked.

### 14.4 Sis / SIS integration

OIS is the *presentation layer* of the SIS:

- SIS ledger stays deterministic; it hashes behaviour, never display strings.
- OIS display strings are substituted at UI/dialogue/render time from `operativeIdentity`.
- Dossier/reputation tiers are derived from SIS state: trust, exposure, witnesses spared/killed, heat, alliances, endings achieved.
- OIS changes nothing about narrative invariants; the dossier may *reflect* them, never alter them.

### 14.5 Content and engineering requirements

- Text tokens: `{OIS_DISPLAY}`, `{OIS_CALLSIGN}`, `{OIS_NICKNAME}`, replaced by the dialogue/UI layer; raw tokens never ship.
- Save schema: `operativeIdentity: { displayName, callsign, nickname, campaignProfile }`.
- Validation: length caps; unicode homoglyph normalisation; banned slur list; fallback to defaults on failure; clear error message, never silent.
- Subtitle caps respected (max ~42 chars/line); callsign caps enforced at entry.
- No custom string is spoken by VO.
- HUD: `SHADOW-07` in the top role line; dossier accessible from pause menu.
- Debug audit (F9) reads the same source; labelled "FIELD REPORT" in-world.
