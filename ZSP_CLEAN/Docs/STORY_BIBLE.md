# ZIMBABWE: THE SHADOW PROTOCOL — Story Bible

**Season 1: The Shadow Protocol**

Everything in this document is fiction. Zimbabwe is used as recognisable geography. Every
named character, unit and organisation is invented, and no depiction is intended as an
allegation about any real person, institution or ethnic group.

---

## Tone and promise

A present-day intelligence thriller in which a disgraced operative discovers that the
country has a shadow government, and that it was built by the people who recruited him.

It is never "Africa is war-torn"; it is never "the West thieves Africa"; it is never a
lecture. It is the story of specific people making judgement calls with incomplete
information, inside a system that punishes honesty.

**Prose style:** short sentences. Concrete detail. Restraint. No speeches.
**Imagery:** long shadows, msasa trees, marble floors, cheap vinyl, corrugated iron,
good whisky, bad coffee, gun oil, diesel, rain that takes its time.
**Themes:** loyalty, compromise, complicity, what "good" costs, what truth costs.

## The disclaimer (shown in-game and on the store page)

> This is a work of fiction. All organisations, units and named individuals are invented.
> Real places are used only as recognisable geography and are not intended to depict real
> institutions or people. Any resemblance to real persons, living or dead, is coincidental.

## The protagonist

### Kuda Moyo, 38
> "The service lied to me. So did the politicians. I'd like to know, for once, whether the truth does."

Former Directorate operative. Left after **Operation Ironwood** — a joint Directorate/PMC
operation he ran on the border that ended with seventeen dead civilians and a sealed
report naming him as the author of the plan. The sealed report was written by Collin
Mavhunga. Kuda carried the shame, left the service, and started driving a truck.

Playable, customisable, not modelled by the player. The player controls clothing, weapons,
equipment and abilities; the face remains the face.

**Visual identity:** worn field jacket; a single unit bracelet he does not wear; a
ring-shaped scar on the left hand that he does not explain; good posture, poor sleep.

## The supporting three

### Nyasha Chikore, 41 — the handler
> "I sent you into Ironwood. I never forgave myself. I was wrong about what it was for."

Former senior analyst. Renowned, decorated, and the architect of the "Shadow Protocol"
archive. For eight months she has been running from her own service after she discovered
what the protocol is actually for. She contacts Kuda because he is the one operative who
was never given the full picture — and because he never believed the briefing anyway.

She is compromised by the SIS when the player leans on her. If she falls, the player can
lose the safehouse, the briefing and her. If she survives, she becomes the conscience of
the story and the most wanted person in the country.

### Tariro Mupfumi, 33 — the journalist
> "I publish the truth. I'm not asking you to become a keyboard."

Investigative reporter at **The Harare Ledger**, a fictional independent paper. She is
unsentimental, organised, and politically useful because nobody wants to charge her. She
is the Expose ending's engine. She is also, in the SIS, a relationship that can be bought,
targeted or lost.

An ally who does not love you. A weapon who does not want to be one.

### Mukoma Simba, 45 — the fixer
> "I'm not loyal to a country. I'm loyal to a few people. You're one of them. That's not the same thing."

Former soldier turned garage mechanic in Mbare. Knows roads, fences, gates and people.
Can get you a car, a gun, a pass, a route. Will not take a briefing; will take a favour,
repaid with interest, eventually. He is the Control ending's engine, and the one person
who could make the player genuinely good — or genuinely complicit.

## The antagonists

### Brigadier-General Farai Gumbo, 58 — the patron
> "Democracy was a holiday we gave the country. Somebody must be the adult."

Founder of **Nightjar Strategic Solutions**, a fictional private military company. Born in
the liberation generation, competent, sincere in his contempt for compromise. He does not
think of himself as a villain; he thinks of himself as a man with an audited mandate to
save his country from its own habits.

His mistake was not cruelty. It was believing that competence deserves power.

### Collin Mavhunga, 49 — the bureaucrat
> "The state is a machine. Machines do not have consciences. They have settings."

Deputy Director of the **Directorate of Strategic Intelligence**. The one who recruited
Kuda, who authorised Ironwood, and who sold its results to Nightjar. He is the opposite of
Gumbo: less a man than a function — patient, procedural, and utterly convinced that
discretion is the highest moral act.

He is the player's original sin.

### Rutendo Chiweshe, 52 — the political cover
Permanent Secretary, Ministry of Interior. Smiling, fastidious, and the author of the
"special liaison budget" that lets Nightjar invoice the state for its own destabilisation.
She is a fiction, but a plausible one. She is the Ledger's easiest catch and the hardest to
prove.

## The witnesses and key characters

### Tendai Chuma, 26 — the courier
Nightjar contract driver. Poor, frightened, redeemable. The SIS turns him into whatever
the player makes of him: informant, liability, casualty.

### Farai "Fingers" Nyandoro, 37 — the bagman
Money launderer and bureau-de-change operator. A man made of paper and excuses. The player
can get his ledger and his testimony, or his body.

### Captain Dereck Mhofu, 35 — the enforcer
Nightjar field commander at the estate. Professional, controlled, and not a fanatic. If the
player treats him as a man, the SIS treats him as a door. If the player treats him as
armour, the SIS treats him as a wall.

  room with the portrait of the old governor.

Later seasons: Nyanga, Lake Chivero, Great Zimbabwe (Nyamande ruins), Kariba, Bulawayo,
the eastern border.

## Season 1: arc and endings

**The Courier's Warning** → **The Ledger** → **The House on Kaguvi Hill**

- **Expose** — the sunlight ending. Gumbo falls; Mavhunga disappears; the state announces
  an internal review. Truth, at the price of safety.
- **Control** — the shadow ending. The protocol changes hands. Calm country, dark hands.
- **Burn** — the ash ending. Nothing left to publish, nothing to leverage, nothing to
  save. Pyrrhic honesty.

## Non-negotiables

1. No real Zimbabwean official, general, minister, police, army, intelligence or
   prosecution is depicted, praised, condemned or implied by the story.
2. No real Zimbabwean institution is named as corrupt.
3. No ethnic group is criminalised, exonerated, or singled out.
4. The state is fictional; the geography is real; the people are invented.
5. The game is pro-Zimbabwean, not anti-anything.
